## PART A: PPT

### Verdict
Favour diagrams. The official SIH template says: "Try to avoid paragraphs and post your idea in points / diagrams / Infographics / pictures." But nobody presents at screening, so the PDF must explain itself: every diagram needs labels and a takeaway headline. Dense paragraphs break the official instruction; icons-only fails "clarity and details in the prescribed format".

### Official rules (same template 2024, 2025, 2026)
- Max 6 slides including the title slide. Use only the provided template, don't change the pointers. Submit as PDF.
- Slides: (1) Title page: PS ID, PS title, theme, category, team ID, team name, exactly as on the portal. (2) Idea title / proposed solution. (3) Technical approach: technologies + methodology (flow charts / images / working prototype). (4) Feasibility and viability: risks and how to overcome them. (5) Impact and benefits. (6) Research and references.
- Official evaluation criteria: novelty, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience, potential for future work. About 4-5 teams per PS go to the finale; the PS owner (ISRO) decides.

### What past winners do (Zaid Sayyed, SIH winner, blog for 2026)
- Title: copy PS fields from the portal character by character.
- Solution: name the specific condition that made the ministry raise the problem.
- Technical: name the real model, library and database. A box saying "AI/ML layer" doesn't count.
- Feasibility: three real risks, each with a mitigation. He calls this the slide that separates decks with the same idea.
- Impact: one number, with the working shown.
- References: named sources with links.
- The one deck measured word by word (labelled a winner): heaviest slide ~130 words, bullets plus one process-flow diagram, no paragraphs.

### Rules of thumb (inference)
1. Headline = the takeaway, max ~12 words ("A voice message crosses campus with zero network"), not "Proposed Solution".
2. 60-100 words per content slide in total, counting diagram labels. No text block over ~25 words.
3. About 50-60% of each content slide is visual.
4. Sequence → flowchart. Layers → architecture diagram. Two options → two columns. Risks → risk/mitigation table.
5. Body text at least 14 pt after PDF export.
6. Blue = Normal, red = SOS, same as the video.
7. Label built vs planned ("Working in prototype" / "Planned").
8. 3-second glance test: show a slide to someone outside the team for 3 s, ask what it said.

### Slide-by-slide diagram plan for iTantra
- **Slide 1 (title):** required fields in a clean left block. Right half: two phone outlines with a dotted voice wave between them and crossed-out tower and Wi-Fi icons. Tagline idea: "Talk when the network can't."
- **Slide 2 (solution, the most important):**
  - Top strip: one-line problem in our words.
  - Centre: message-journey flowchart, one icon and 2-4 words per step: Speak → Speech-to-text (on device) → Compress to low bitrate → Send over Bluetooth / phone hops → Receiver → Text-to-speech plays it. Badge: "no internet · no SIM · no Wi-Fi". Tested hops solid, untested relay hops dashed and labelled "designed".
  - Bottom: Normal (blue) vs SOS (red) two-column table: who receives (one trusted contact / anyone nearby), relays (pass it on, content not shown / Accept or Decline and reply), use (check-ins / emergencies). Only say "can't read it" if encryption is actually implemented.
  - Small "why existing options fail" table: WhatsApp/SMS, satellite phones, walkie-talkies, iTantra vs works without network / voice-first / no extra hardware. Only features we can cite.
- **Slide 3 (technical):** left: stacked architecture (UI with push-to-talk and SOS → STT engine → message packer → transport → relay / store-and-forward → TTS), real library name on each box. Right: 2-3 real app screenshots with "Working in prototype" badges. Optional "Measured so far" strip, real numbers only.
- **Slide 4 (feasibility):** risk → mitigation table with status dots. Candidates: short Bluetooth range → hops + store-and-forward; battery drain → duty-cycled scanning; STT accuracy in noise or regional languages → model choice + fallback to raw audio; fake SOS → rate limits + Accept/Decline; few phones nearby → message waits and forwards later. Side box: technical / economic / operational feasibility, one line each.
- **Slide 5 (impact):** a 3-4 frame scenario strip (landslide cuts a tower → stranded person speaks → message hops via a rescuer's phone → relief camp hears it) plus beneficiary icons (disaster victims, rescue teams, remote workers / ISRO field sites). One sourced number with working shown.
- **Slide 6 (references):** two columns: technical sources and libraries; problem evidence (e.g. NDMA / government reports on network outages in disasters), with links.

## PART B: VIDEO

### What judges and platforms want
- Devpost: most hackathons want under 3 minutes; judges watch videos back to back; explain what the app does in the first few seconds; show it working, not slides.
- ETHGlobal: 2-4 minutes; backstory max ~20 s; skip waiting; don't speed up the demo to fit time; don't use a text-to-speech voice for narration (the app's own TTS output is fine).
- Google Solution Challenge: max 120 s; judged on whether it shows an actual working app (not a mockup) and evidence of testing with real users.
- Wistia (marketing audiences): biggest drop-off is in the first seconds; put what matters in the first shots.
- Cold opens: show an experience first, explain after. If the opening can be summarised as "someone explains the situation", it isn't doing its job.

### What "better intro" means here (inference)
1. Show the problem in pictures in the first 5-8 s.
2. Name the product and its one promise by ~15-20 s.
3. Show proof early (a real phone in airplane mode doing something real).
4. Hand over to the demo by ~25-30 s, not 56 s.
5. Team and PS ID: one line plus a lower-third; full credits at the end.
6. Honest: nothing suggests something was tested when it wasn't.
7. Legible and audible: captions, big panel text, clean voice.

### Three intro options
- **A, "Silent campus" (chosen; shot on 2 Oct):** empty campus → walk along the path → airplane mode close-up → she walks in and looks around → thumb on push-to-talk → over-the-shoulder → short to-camera pitch with panel → "Let's see it work." Low effort, mostly editing. Keep each shot ≤3 s so the opening doesn't drag.
- **B, "Do it with me":** she switches on airplane mode to camera: "Turn off your mobile data. Wi-Fi. Everything." / "Now try sending a voice message." / "In a flood or a landslide, this is all you have." then the pitch. Mostly talking; depends on energetic delivery. Not shot.
- **C, "Proof first":** opens on Yash's phone in airplane mode receiving a message and playing it via TTS, then "So how did this arrive?" Strongest, but needs a genuine, uncut receive clip with airplane mode visible. Not shot; possible later only with a real receive.
